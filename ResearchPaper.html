01 / ABSTRACT

Abstract

Can a leakage-aware machine-learning ranking model help SEO and content reviewers prioritize pages for human review when review capacity is limited? We evaluate a Random Forest ranking approach across 30,000 anonymized content-page observations using search, traffic, engagement, position, and freshness signals, evaluated with an 80/20 client-grouped split and NDCG@20 as the ranking metric. After a target-feature leakage pathway was audited and removed, the corrected model achieved an NDCG@20 of 0.857936 versus 0.368183 for the heuristic baseline on the same held-out clients (+0.489753 delta). This result provides directional evidence that flexible modeling improves ordering against a constructed opportunity proxy, rather than evidence of guaranteed traffic growth or causal search engine mechanics. The resulting queue is therefore implemented as a decision-support system featuring diagnostic reason codes and strict no-go governance guardrails rather than automated content publishing.

02 / INTRODUCTION

Introduction & Problem Statement

When editorial and SEO teams manage thousands of published URLs with finite human review capacity, the primary bottleneck is not generating metrics—it is deciding which pages deserve editorial inspection first.

This research investigates whether machine learning can synthesize multidimensional search demand, user engagement, and freshness signals into a reliable triage queue. The concrete decision supported is strictly operational: Which content pages should a reviewer inspect first when review capacity is limited?

We focus on the CTR / Engagement Opportunity Scoring lane. Traditional SEO heuristics rely on isolated bivariate thresholds (such as ranking position vs. raw CTR), which fail to capture nonlinear feature interactions, traffic scale, and multi-signal engagement patterns.

Crucially, the operational cost of errors in content optimization is asymmetric. False positives waste scarce specialist hours, while unwarranted automated interventions (such as bulk metadata modifications or automated page rewrites) risk degrading organic rankings and user experience. Consequently, this system is architected strictly for human-in-the-loop decision support with robust governance guardrails.

04 / METHODOLOGY

Methodology & Validation Design

The optimization task is framed as a continuous opportunity ranking problem. Rather than predicting binary conversions, the model ranks pages by estimated improvement potential.

Target Proxy Formulation

Because direct post-intervention outcomes are unobserved in historical logs, we formulate a transparent composite proxy score combining engagement depth and momentum:

Target Proxy = 0.5 × percentile(engagement_rate) + 0.2 × percentile(scroll_rate) + 0.3 × percentile(trend_pct)

To eliminate cross-partition contamination, the proxy percentile transforms are computed independently within train and test splits.

Heuristic Baseline

The Week-4 benchmark heuristic establishes position-bucket expected CTRs, calculates the non-negative CTR gap, and combines it with log-transformed search demand:

Baseline Score = 0.7 × normalized(CTR gap) + 0.3 × normalized(log(search_volume + 1))

Leakage Remediation & Split Protocol

Initial Week-5 iterations achieved an unrealistic NDCG@20 of 0.9989 due to target-feature leakage (supplying engagement_rate, scroll_rate, and trend_pct directly into the feature matrix). Our Week-6 audit purged these fields from the model inputs.

Evaluation uses GroupShuffleSplit (test_size = 0.20, random_state = 42) grouped by client ID. This partitions the 25,188 rows into 20,624 training pages (24 clients) and 4,564 test pages (7 clients) with exactly 0 client overlap.

06 / LIMITATIONS

Limitations & Honest Framing

1. Non-Causal Associations

Feature importances represent model associations under historical data distributions. A high importance for engaged_sessions_90d does not imply that artificially manipulating session metrics will causally improve organic rankings.

2. Constructed Proxy Target

The relevance label is an engineered composite metric, not a verified future business outcome. Model scores indicate prioritization relative to this proxy rather than forecasted revenue or traffic gains.

3. Lack of Temporal Splitting

Due to the absence of timestamped records in the dataset, holdout evaluation is cross-sectional across clients rather than forward-looking across time horizons.

4. Generalization Scope

Performance reflects the 31 modeled client portfolios. Results may differ across niche verticals, non-English search markets, or radical search engine algorithm updates.

Operating Principle: Machine learning scores serve as prioritization signals to guide human editorial expertise, not automated decision engines.

Strict Governance No-Go Rules

To prevent algorithmic harm, the following automated actions are explicitly prohibited:

  • No Automatic Publishing: Content modifications must be reviewed and staged by human editors.
  • No Automated Rewriting: Generative rewriting without editorial oversight is disallowed.
  • No Bulk URL Deletion or Redirection: Prevents destructive loss of indexation equity.
  • No Automated Canonical / Noindex Directives: Technical search settings require specialist validation.
  • No Guaranteed Traffic Claims: Rankings reflect opportunity scoring, not traffic warranties.
